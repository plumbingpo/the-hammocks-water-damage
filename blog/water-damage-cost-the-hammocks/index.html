How Much Does Water Damage Cost in The Hammocks?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in The Hammocks?

The Quick Answer

In The Hammocks, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. At the other end, heavy flooding or sewage with reconstruction work can go well beyond $10,000.

Those numbers describe what is typical nationally; they are not a quote. Local labor rates and material prices in The Hammocks, FL move the numbers up or down. An exact price requires someone to inspect the home in person and take moisture readings.

Pricing by Category of Water

Water type is normally the starting point when a restoration job is priced. The industry sorts water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. A job with dirty water calls for extra protective gear, more disinfecting and more material taken out.

Depending on which category applies, US cleanup costs are usually in the area of $3 to $7.50 a square foot. The table below shows common rates.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Supply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterFailed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterStorm surge, flooding rivers, sewageRoughly $7 to $7.50

A loss does not always stay in one category. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

What Drives the Price

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. When a job is priced, the contractor considers the following.

  • Area affected. More wet square footage means more labor, more equipment and more days on site.
  • Water category. Clean water is the cheapest to handle. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. Given time, water works its way into wall cavities and subfloors. A leak that runs all weekend while you are gone costs far more than one caught in an hour.
  • Materials. Concrete and tile are easy to dry. Insulation, drywall, carpet pad and hardwood frequently have to be replaced or dried with special methods.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. Drying is one cost. Afterward comes a second cost for new flooring, drywall, paint and cabinets, which on larger jobs often exceeds the first.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. In general the drying phase lasts 3 to 5 days.

A daily rate is common for dehumidifiers and air movers. If your house is dry after three days, you pay less than if it needs six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in The Hammocks. Use the numbers to get oriented and not as a firm price.

  • Burst pipe. Drying and cleanup frequently total $1,500 to $5,000, and the burst pipe repair is on top of that. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. Found early, a leak from a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine is often $1,000 to $4,000. When a slow drip goes unnoticed below cabinets, concealed damage raises the price.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Basement water removal in a finished basement sits at the high end.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Because sewage cleanup falls under Category 3, crews disinfect the whole area and take out porous materials.
  • Roof leak. Often $1,000 to $5,000 for drying and repairing ceilings, insulation and walls, not counting the roof fix.

Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage in The Hammocks?

The answer turns on your policy and on what caused the damage. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

A leak that developed slowly is usually excluded. If a fitting dripped for months, an insurer may call it a maintenance issue. Outside flooding, for instance from storm surge or a river that rises, is usually not covered without separate flood insurance. Sewer backup often needs its own add-on.

Check your policy or call your agent before you assume anything. The deductible is a factor as well. On a small job, the repair may cost less than the deductible, and a claim may not make sense.

Keeping Your Costs Under Control

What happens early, within the first couple of days, determines the bulk of a water damage bill. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. More material gets wet with each passing hour.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Shoot video and pictures first, while everything is still in place and nothing has been tossed. Keep receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. A job with active growth takes more days and costs more money.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Stay out of standing water while the electricity is on, and keep away from water that might have sewage in it. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Getting a Real Number

A national range helps with budgeting, yet no house is the average house. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.

If you need water damage restoration in The Hammocks, FL, the best time to ask for that inspection is now, while the damage is still small.
