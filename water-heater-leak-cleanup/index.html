Water Heater Leak Cleanup in The Hammocks: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our The Hammocks crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
How We Fix It

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.
