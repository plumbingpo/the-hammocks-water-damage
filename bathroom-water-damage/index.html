Bathroom Water Damage: What The Hammocks Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. We help The Hammocks homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

The Room Below

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.
