Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in The Hammocks

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

What It Looks Like

  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Our Process

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.
