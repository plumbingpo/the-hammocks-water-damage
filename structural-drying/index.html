Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The Hammocks Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

When to Call for Structural Drying in The Hammocks

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Warped casing or baseboard where water reached the bottom of the wall

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
