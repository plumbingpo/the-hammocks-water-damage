Crawl Space Water Removal: What The Hammocks Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Warning Signs

  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.
