Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in The Hammocks

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. Success depends on a slow, even drying rate that draws water from the flooring and from the subfloor beneath it.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Expect it to run a week or more, which is longer than drywall or carpet needs. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

What It Looks Like

  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Mildew smell coming up through the boards

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Take Baseline Readings

We take readings on the boards, the subfloor and unaffected flooring nearby for comparison. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. The faster water stops feeding the seams, the less the boards swell.

3

Install the Mat System

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Monitor and Advise

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. Our final advice is one of three outcomes, no further work, swapping a few boards, or refinishing the floor.

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Sanding Too Early

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Drying Too Fast

High heat or too much airflow pulls moisture from the surface faster than the core can follow. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Damage Beyond the Room

Water under a floor travels. It can reach under cabinets, wall plates and into neighboring rooms, or drip through to the ceiling of the level beneath.

Rusting Fasteners

Nails and staples that stay wet begin to rust and lose holding power. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.
