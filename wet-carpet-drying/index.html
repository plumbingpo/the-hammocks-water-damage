Wet Carpet Drying in The Hammocks

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. Our team in The Hammocks extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Subfloor Damage

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
