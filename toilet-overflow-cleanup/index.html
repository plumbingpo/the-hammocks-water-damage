Toilet Overflow Cleanup in The Hammocks: What to Know

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

The first thing our The Hammocks team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. A stuck valve can be worked around by taking off the tank lid and lifting the float, which stops the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • The water category, since contaminated overflows need more labor, protection and disinfection.
  • Whether the toilet or vanity has to be pulled to dry and clean beneath it.
  • Whether the overflow stayed in the bathroom or reached carpet, closets or a hall outside it.
  • Bathroom flooring material, since some floors dry in place and others must be taken up.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • A stuck flapper or fill valve that keeps the tank running without stopping.
  • A main sewer line blockage that backs waste up through the lowest toilet.
  • A worn wax ring that lets water out at the base each time the toilet is flushed.
  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Bacterial Contamination

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. Paint may look normal even when the wall is damp several inches up from the floor.

Lingering Odor

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.
