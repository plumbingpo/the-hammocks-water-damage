Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in The Hammocks: What to Know

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Do not wait for the water to drain away by itself.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • An intake screen packed with mud and stones so the pump cannot draw water.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • A power outage during the storm with no battery backup on the pump.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Boxes and Stored Items

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.

Bottom Plates and Studs

The wood plate at the base of each wall sits right on the slab and soaks up water first. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

How We Fix It

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Get the Water Out

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How deep the water got and how much of the basement it covered.
  • The time that passed before the water was pumped out and drying started.
  • Whether a temporary pump is needed to keep groundwater down during the work.
  • A finished basement, which adds carpet, trim and drywall removal.
