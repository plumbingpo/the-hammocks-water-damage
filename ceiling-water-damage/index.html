Ceiling Water Damage Repair in The Hammocks

The stain overhead comes at the end of the story, not the beginning. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Warning Signs

  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Electricity Overhead

Can lights, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are located exactly where the water is. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

Our Process

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.
