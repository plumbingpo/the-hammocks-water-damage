A dry house is not yet a finished house. Once the moisture readings are back to normal, there are still cut walls, missing flooring and bare subfloor to deal with. Water damage repair is the rebuild. Our team in The Hammocks replaces what could not be saved and matches it to what was there before, so the room looks like a room again and not a job site.

The scope of repairs varies a lot. On a small job it may be a few feet of drywall, new baseboard and paint. Large jobs can include insulation, whole drywall sheets, subfloor, laminate or hardwood, cabinet boxes, doors and trim. Where we can, replacements are like for like, and if no exact match exists you will hear it plainly and make the choice.

Dry first, then cover: that order never changes. A moisture meter has to show normal levels in the subfloor and framing before new material is installed. Cover wood that is still damp, and mold grows unseen behind the new paint. Before we begin, a schedule and scope in writing tell you which items are being rebuilt and the sequence.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Hold off on heavy traffic and wet cleaning until new adhesives, caulk and paint have cured.
  • Rebuilt bathroom walls last longer when the exhaust fan runs through and after each shower.
  • Store pictures of the finished work alongside the scope for future reference.
  • Laundry rooms, bathrooms and kitchens should be rebuilt with moisture resistant wallboard and proper backer board.
  • Keep leftover flooring, tile and paint details so future repairs can be matched.
  • In case the insurance company asks down the road, photograph anything damaged while it is still in place.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Walking over the formerly wet area, you feel soft spots in the floor.
  • Seams are opening, or edges are curling and swelling, on vinyl plank or laminate.
  • Wall cavities remain open where drywall was removed for drying.
  • Door casings and baseboards have warped or pulled off the wall surface.
  • Since the water damage, certain doors drag on the jamb or miss the latch.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • Where water ran down a wall, the paint has blistered, peeled or turned brown.

Water Damage Repair, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage repair from start to finish:

1

Confirm It Is Dry

Final moisture readings are taken on the subfloor, framing and any drywall left in place. Until the readings are normal for every material, no reconstruction happens, and no moisture gets trapped.

2

Put the Scope in Writing

You receive a room by room list of what will be replaced, the materials we plan to use and the expected schedule. If a claim is open, we line the scope up with the adjuster.

3

Reconstruct and Install

We refill open cavities with insulation, hang, tape and finish drywall, and repair the subfloor. Flooring, cabinets and trim are then installed in the order that protects the finished surfaces.

4

Finish and Walk Through

The last stage covers texture matching, primer, paint and putting hardware, doors and baseboards back. Together we then walk through each room, and every listed item is corrected before the job is closed.
