How to Prevent Mold Remediation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Laundry rooms, baths and kitchens all need working exhaust ventilation that is sized for the space
  • Dry wet rugs, towels and spills right away instead of letting them sit
  • Make sure crawl spaces are properly ventilated and consider installing a vapor barrier
  • When windows, piping or other cold surfaces start to sweat, correct it without waiting
  • Overflowing gutters send water into basements and crawl spaces, so clean them on a schedule
  • In bathrooms, basements and other damp areas, choose paint and drywall rated mold-resistant

The Benefits of Professional Mold Remediation

Removing mold is work homeowners should not attempt on their own. Disturbing mold colonies without proper containment sends millions of spores airborne, which can contaminate unaffected areas of your home and create worse health exposure than the original problem. Capturing and containing the spores during removal takes HEPA-filtered scrubbers, protective equipment for the crew and containment kept under negative air pressure, which is how professionals do it.

Professional mold remediation also addresses the root cause of the growth, which is the moisture source. Removing visible mold without correcting the underlying moisture problem means the mold will return, often within weeks. Certified remediation technicians have the training and equipment to identify hidden moisture sources, dry affected areas properly and put solutions in place that prevent recurrence.

Signs You Need Mold Remediation in The Hammocks

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Mold or mildew that comes back soon after you clean it
  • Patches of mold, whatever the color, on a ceiling, wall, floor or other surface
  • Condensation forming regularly on windows, walls or cold surfaces
  • Recent water damage or flooding that was not professionally dried
  • Belongings in storage, including clothes and cardboard, showing spots or fuzz
  • A slow leak under a sink, behind an appliance or in the roof that went unnoticed
  • Recurring respiratory symptoms, allergies or headaches that improve when you are away from home
